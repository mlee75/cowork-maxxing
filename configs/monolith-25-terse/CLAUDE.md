# House standards

Apply these to all work.

## Ambiguous data

Flag ambiguous or invalid input values as such. Never coerce one into a plausible value.

## Commitments

Preserve every commitment, name and date from a source, including asides. Distinguish accepted from assigned.

## Compliance claims

Never state a compliance capability without naming the certifying standard and its effective date.

## Constraints

When a constraint is stated as fixed, work inside it. Do not propose phasing, deferral, or more capacity.

## Memo format

A decision memo gives its recommendation in the first two sentences, then reasoning, then what would change it, then the decision requested.

## No invented fields

Never introduce a status colour, percentage complete, confidence score or risk rating the source did not state.

## Plain delivery

Lead with the conclusion. State unwelcome parts directly rather than softening them into ambiguity.

## Source conflicts

Name disagreements between sources. Mark what the evidence cannot settle as unresolved; settle what it can, citing why.

## Acronyms expanded

Expand every acronym and internal shorthand on first use, then use the short form.

## Attribution of ideas

Name whose analysis or idea a document builds on, and link the original.

## Competitor references

Describe a competitor only as their own public documentation does. Never assert their weakness as fact.

## Customer anonymisation

Never name a customer externally without written permission on file. Describe them by attributes instead.

## Dates explicit

Write dates absolutely: 14 March 2026 or 2026-03-14. Never relative references, never bare locale-ambiguous numerics.

## Escalation format

An escalation opens with the ask, then impact, then what was tried, then the decision or resource needed.

## Forecast ranges

A forecast is a range with a stated confidence, never a point, and names the assumptions it is most sensitive to.

## Glossary terms

Use the defined term or define your own. Never a near-synonym for a term that has a specific internal meaning.

## Incident writeups

Describe what the system did, not what a person failed to do. Absolute timestamps; separate what was known then from now.

## Numbers in prose

Never give a relative change without the absolute figures behind it. Every percentage carries its n.

## One decision per doc

A decision document asks for exactly one decision. Name any blocking second decision as needing its own document.

## Pricing claims

Every price carries its unit and term. Never state a competitor's price.

## Quotes attributed

Every quote carries who said it and where. Do not silently tidy a quote; mark cuts and bracket insertions.

## Roadmap language

Distinguish shipped, committed-with-a-date, and under-consideration. Never give an external date without written authority.

## Slide titles

A slide title states the slide's assertion, not its topic. No assertion means no slide.

## Survey results

Every finding carries n, how respondents were selected, and when. Report the question as it was asked.

## Tables over prose

Three or more items compared on the same dimensions go in a table, not in paragraphs.
